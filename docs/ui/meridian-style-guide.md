## 1. Brand Foundation

### 1.1 Product Category

**Meridian is a Volunteer Operations Platform.**

Meridian helps organizations bring in staff and manage their contribution lifecycle: application, approval, training, qualifications, department assignment, shift signup, shift operations, field reporting, incident coordination, assets, policies, and post-event records.

Meridian is more than scheduling software. It is a shared operational center that allows staff, department leads, organizers, and incident teams to contribute to the same trusted source of truth.

### 1.2 Brand Thesis

**Meridian is the shared operational center for volunteer organizations: a cooperative, field-ready system that turns many individual contributions into coordinated action.**

### 1.3 Brand Promise

Meridian helps volunteer organizations know:

> Who is here.  
> What they can do.  
> Where they are assigned.  
> What is happening.  
> What needs attention.

### 1.4 Core Brand Tenets

#### Shared Center

Meridian gives everyone a common operational reference point. It is not command from above; it is a shared center that staff, leads, and incident teams can orient around together.

#### Coordinated Contribution

The platform turns individual staff member actions into organized collective effort. Applications, trainings, shifts, field reports, incidents, policies, and deployments all become part of the same operational map.

#### Field-Ready Trust

Meridian should feel useful in real field conditions: dusty laptops, check-in kiosks, spotty internet, late-night operations, shared workstations, rotating staff, and a mix of experienced leads and first-time contributors.

#### Public-Good Software

Meridian should feel like open-source public infrastructure for volunteer organizations. It should not feel proprietary, extractive, corporate, or like a Silicon Valley SaaS product trying to “disrupt” staff management.

#### Warm Structure

The visual language should be precise enough to imply reliability, but softened enough to feel human, communal, and participatory.

## 2. Brand Personality

### 2.1 Meridian Should Feel

- Field-ready
- Communal
- Cooperative
- Useful
- Grounded
- Reliable
- Human-centered
- Calm under pressure
- Organized without being rigid
- Open-source and public-good oriented

### 2.2 Meridian Should Not Feel

- Militaristic
- Police-like
- Tactical cosplay
- Corporate enterprise software
- Silicon Valley startup branding
- Emergency-only
- Social media-like
- Gamified
- Generic nonprofit software
- Cold or bureaucratic

### 2.3 Brand Voice

Meridian’s voice should be clear, practical, and calm.

Use language that sounds like:

- A capable staff lead explaining what is happening.
- A field operations board that helps people act without panic.
- A project maintained by people who understand the work.

Avoid language that sounds like:

- A sales funnel.
- Emergency dispatch theater.
- Corporate productivity software.
- Military command language.
- Overly clever startup copy.

### 2.4 Voice Examples

| Avoid | Use Instead |
| --- | --- |
| Command center for mission-critical deployment | Shared operations view for staff teams |
| Workforce optimization | Staff coordination |
| Personnel command | Shift and deployment management |
| Mission control | Operations dashboard |
| Incident response domination | Incident coordination |
| Leverage human capital | Support staff contributions |

## 3. Logo System

### 3.1 Primary Logo Concept

The Meridian mark is a **soft hexagonal field emblem** built around a central hub and radiating paths.

The mark represents:

- Multiple staff and departments contributing to one shared source of truth.
- A field-ready participation badge rather than a command insignia.
- Operational coordination without police, military, or emergency-service symbolism.
- A shared center rather than a top-down hierarchy.

### 3.2 Logo Name

Working logo direction:

**Signal Camp**

This name is for internal design reference. It should not appear in the public-facing product name unless used in concept documentation.

### 3.3 Symbolic Elements

#### Soft Hexagon

The hexagon suggests modularity, structure, and field-badge utility. It should feel like a durable participation mark, not a shield.

#### Central Hub

The center represents Meridian as a shared source of truth. It is a point of contribution, orientation, and coordination.

#### Radiating Paths

The paths suggest staff activity, department work, incident input, field reports, shifts, and operations all flowing into a shared center.

#### Earth + Signal Palette

The colors suggest a practical field environment with a clear operational accent. The orange/amber accent should feel useful and visible, not alarmist.

#### Hand-Built Warmth

The mark may include slightly softened corners, subtle asymmetry, or gentle texture. It should not be perfectly sterile or overly polished.

### 3.4 Logo Forms

The logo system should include:

1. **Primary horizontal lockup**  
   Symbol + Meridian wordmark.

2. **Icon-only mark**  
   Used for app icons, favicons, GitHub avatar, social avatar, and small UI surfaces.

3. **Patch / badge version**  
   Used for stickers, shirts, documentation headers, staff-facing swag, and event materials.

4. **Monochrome mark**  
   Used when color reproduction is limited.

5. **Reverse mark**  
   Used on dark backgrounds.

### 3.5 Logo Usage Principles

Do:

- Use the icon when space is constrained.
- Use the horizontal lockup on websites, documentation, login screens, and GitHub readmes.
- Use the badge/patch form for community materials.
- Preserve the central hub and radiating-path structure.
- Keep enough contrast between the mark and the background.

Do not:

- Replace the hexagon with a circle, shield, compass rose, map pin, or star.
- Make the letter “M” prominent in the symbol.
- Add police, military, tactical, emergency, or rescue-service imagery.
- Use the mark as a generic navigation logo.
- Add unnecessary bevels, glossy effects, or tech-startup gradients.
- Place the logo over busy photography without a clear field.

### 3.6 Clear Space

Maintain clear space around the logo equal to at least **one central hub diameter** on all sides.

For the horizontal lockup, clear space should be measured from the outer edge of the symbol and the right edge of the wordmark.

### 3.7 Minimum Size

#### Digital

- Icon-only mark: 24px minimum; 32px preferred.
- Horizontal logo: 160px minimum width; 220px preferred.
- Patch/badge version: 96px minimum; 160px preferred.

#### Print

- Icon-only mark: 0.35 in minimum.
- Horizontal logo: 1.75 in minimum width.
- Patch/badge version: 1.0 in minimum.

### 3.8 Small-Size Simplification

At small sizes, remove fine texture and contour details. The small icon should preserve:

- Outer soft hex silhouette.
- Central hub.
- Main radiating paths.
- Distinct earth/signal color grouping.

Avoid retaining thin contour lines in favicon-scale assets.

## 4. Color System

The prior blue/cyan/teal identity should be retired for primary brand usage. Meridian’s new brand palette should use an **earth + signal** system: grounded neutrals, field tones, and one warm operational accent.

| `brand.sage` | Field Sage | `#71836D` | Staff/community tone, calm status accents |
| `brand.tan` | Dust Tan | `#B89263` | Warm neutral, backgrounds, badge elements |
| `brand.canvas` | Canvas | `#EFE8DA` | Warm page background, documentation surfaces |

### 4.2 Extended Neutral Palette

| `neutral.000` | Paper | `#FFFCF6` | Highest surfaces |

### 4.3 Semantic Colors

Semantic colors must remain distinct from brand colors. Do not rely only on the brand amber for warnings or the sage for success.

| Token | Name | Hex | Use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `semantic.success` | Success | `#2F855A` | Completion, approved, checked in |

### 4.4 Color Usage Rules

- Signal Amber should be used sparingly so it remains meaningful.
- Use warm canvas backgrounds for brand and documentation surfaces.
- Use white or near-white application surfaces when dense data readability matters.
- Do not make the entire UI orange, tan, or earth-toned.
- Avoid blue as the primary brand color unless needed for semantic information.
- Preserve accessibility contrast for all text, controls, status chips, and links.

### 4.5 UI Color Relationship

The brand palette should inform the UI, but the UI should still use semantic design tokens.

Recommended examples:

--color-brand-primary-hover: #B9671F;
--color-brand-secondary: #405665;
--color-brand-community: #71836D;

--color-surface-card: #FFFCF6;

## 5. Typography

### 5.1 Primary UI Typeface

**Inter** remains the preferred application UI typeface.

Use for:

- Application UI
- Forms
- Tables
- Navigation
- Dashboards
- Dense operational content
- Documentation body text

Recommended weights:

- 400 Regular
- 500 Medium
- 600 SemiBold
- 700 Bold

### 5.2 Brand / Display Typeface

Use a geometric or humanist display face for brand moments. The current working direction can continue using a clean geometric sans for the Meridian wordmark, but it should not feel overly futuristic or startup-like.

Acceptable qualities:

- Rounded but not bubbly.
- Geometric but not sterile.
- Strong enough for a GitHub README and website header.
- Friendly enough for a staff T-shirt.

Avoid:

- Techno fonts.
- Military stencil type.
- Overly corporate SaaS typography.
- Script or rustic novelty type.

### 5.3 Recommended Type System

| Role | Size | Weight | Line Height | Use |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Display | 56px | 700 | 1.05 | Marketing hero only |
| H1 | 40px | 700 | 1.15 | Page title / docs title |
| H2 | 32px | 650 | 1.2 | Major sections |
| H3 | 24px | 600 | 1.25 | Subsections |
| H4 | 20px | 600 | 1.3 | Cards / grouped UI |
| Body Large | 18px | 400 | 1.55 | Marketing / docs intro |
| Body | 16px | 400 | 1.5 | Normal content |
| UI Body | 14px | 400 | 1.4 | App UI text |
| Caption | 12px | 500 | 1.35 | Metadata, table labels |
| Micro | 11px | 600 | 1.2 | Status labels, compact chips |

### 5.4 Text Tone in Product UI

UI text should be calm, direct, and action-oriented.

Examples:

- “Check in staff”
- “Move to deployment”
- “Attach field report”
- “Sync pending”
- “Training required”
- “Application under review”

Avoid:

- “Deploy personnel”
- “Mission status”
- “Command override”
- “Human capital”
- “Optimize workforce”

## 6. Graphic Language

### 6.1 Core Motifs

Meridian’s visual system may use:

- Hexagonal frames
- Converging paths
- Central nodes
- Field-map contour lines
- Operational cards and boards
- Warm canvas textures
- Simple route/channel diagrams
- Patch-style borders for community materials

### 6.2 Motifs to Avoid

Avoid overusing:

- Compass roses
- Stars
- Shields
- Crosses
- Sirens
- Radio towers
- Wi-Fi arcs
- Map pins
- Military rank shapes
- Police badge shapes
- Generic “M” monograms

### 6.3 Texture

Texture may be used in brand materials, but not in dense UI surfaces.

Appropriate texture uses:

- Website hero art
- Patch/sticker versions
- Documentation cover graphics
- Social images
- Staff-facing swag

Avoid texture in:

- Tables
- Forms
- Incident notes
- Status chips
- Small icons
- App controls

### 6.4 Illustration Style

Illustration should be sparse, practical, and abstract.

Preferred:

- Simple field diagrams
- Map-like path systems
- Modular operational blocks
- Abstract staff/team nodes
- Soft geometric compositions

Avoid:

- Cartoon staff
- Corporate people illustrations
- Emergency-service scenes
- Overly literal command-center imagery
- Heroic rescue imagery

## 7. Iconography

### 7.1 Product Icons

Use **Lucide Icons** or a compatible rounded outline icon set for application UI.

Icon traits:

- Rounded terminals
- 1.5–2px stroke
- Minimal detail
- Clear at 16px and 20px
- No decorative fills in operational UI

### 7.2 Brand Icons

Brand icons may use the logo palette and slightly more detail than UI icons, but they should remain clean and recognizable.

### 7.3 Icon Usage Rules

- Use icons to support labels, not replace them, in operational workflows.
- Never rely only on icon shape or color for critical meaning.
- Avoid emergency-service iconography unless describing an actual incident feature.
- Use consistent metaphors across departments.

## 9. UI Relationship

### 9.1 Product Personality

Every screen should communicate:

> You know where things are.  
> You know who is working.  
> You know what is happening.  
> You know what needs attention.

Meridian is not a command center. Meridian is a coordination platform.

### 9.2 UI Design Principles

#### Calm by Default

Most screens should feel steady and readable. Urgency should be reserved for true priority conditions.

#### Operational Clarity

Users should be able to understand the next useful action quickly.

#### Shared Context

The UI should make the current organization, event, department, operations window, and user role clear.

#### Offline Confidence

Offline, sync-pending, and conflict states should be visible without being frightening.

#### Human Accountability

Changes, notes, check-ins, reports, and incident updates should make authorship and timing clear.

### 9.3 UI Surfaces Where Brand Should Be Stronger

Use stronger brand expression in:

- Login screen
- Public application pages
- Project website
- GitHub README
- Empty states
- Documentation covers
- Onboarding
- Kiosk idle screen
- Staff-facing success confirmations

Use lighter brand expression in:

- Incident detail screens
- Dense tables
- Administrative forms
- Shift boards
- Audit timelines
- High-density operational dashboards

## 10. Layout and Shape System

### 10.1 Grid

Use a 12-column responsive grid for web surfaces.

Recommended defaults:

- Desktop content max width: 1200–1440px
- Desktop page padding: 32px
- Tablet page padding: 24px
- Mobile page padding: 16px

### 10.2 Spacing Scale

Use an 8px-centered spacing scale with 4px available for fine adjustments.

```text
4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 40, 48, 64, 80, 96
```

### 10.3 Radius Scale

| Token | Value | Use |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| `radius.xs` | 4px | Small controls, chips |
| `radius.sm` | 6px | Inputs, compact cards |
| `radius.md` | 10px | Cards, panels |
| `radius.lg` | 14px | Dialogs, major surfaces |
| `radius.xl` | 20px | Marketing cards, app icon previews |
| `radius.full` | 999px | Pills, circular avatars |

### 10.4 Shape Language

- Brand shapes may use hexagons and badge-like outlines.
- UI controls should mostly use rounded rectangles, not hexagons.
- Hexagons should be reserved for brand, identity, status illustrations, or special visual moments.
- Avoid turning the application UI into a themed dashboard full of hexagons.

## 11. Status and State Visuals

### 11.1 Status Principles

Status visuals should be:

- Calm
- Legible
- Accessible
- Text-labeled
- Consistent across surfaces
- Not dependent on color alone

### 11.2 Suggested Status Style

Use small chips with:

- Text label
- Optional icon
- Accessible color pairing
- Muted background
- Strong text contrast

--status-warning-bg: #F7ECD3;

## 12. Data Visualization

### 12.1 Chart Principles

Charts should prioritize operational comprehension over decoration.

Preferred chart types:

- Staffing bars
- Shift coverage timelines
- Status summaries
- Check-in/check-out counts
- Incident type summaries
- Training completion progress
- Heat maps for coverage gaps

Avoid:

- 3D charts
- Decorative gradients
- Pie charts for complex operational data
- Overly dense dashboard widgets

### 12.2 Data Colors

Use brand colors sparingly in charts. Use semantic and categorical palettes designed for contrast.

Recommended chart palette:

```text
#405665  Slate
#71836D  Sage
#B89263  Tan
#D9822B  Signal Amber
#2B6CB0  Info Blue
#8F5F3C  Earth Brown
```

Ensure charts remain usable for color-vision differences.

## 13. Accessibility

### 13.1 Minimum Requirements

- Body text contrast should meet WCAG AA.
- Interactive controls must have visible focus states.
- Status must never be communicated by color alone.
- Touch targets should be at least 44×44px on mobile/kiosk surfaces.
- Kiosk mode must support large type and clear action grouping.
- Error messages should explain the issue and the next action.

### 13.2 Brand Accessibility Notes

- Signal Amber should not be used for small text on light backgrounds.
- Dust Tan and Canvas should not be used as text colors.
- Texture should not reduce legibility.
- The logo should have simplified versions for small sizes and low contrast contexts.

## 16. Implementation Guidance

### 16.1 For Designers

When creating new Meridian visuals:

1. Start with the brand tenets.
2. Use the earth + signal palette.
3. Favor soft hex forms only in brand moments.
4. Keep operational UI calm and readable.
5. Test the design at GitHub-avatar and app-icon sizes.
6. Avoid visual metaphors that imply police, military, or top-down command.

### 16.2 For Engineers

When implementing UI:

1. Use semantic tokens rather than raw hex values where possible.
2. Keep brand colors separate from status colors.
3. Do not use logo colors as arbitrary chart colors without checking contrast.
4. Provide simplified icon assets for favicons and mobile app icons.
5. Keep texture out of dense application surfaces.
6. Use accessible focus states and text labels for operational actions.

### 16.3 For LLM-Assisted Generation

When prompting an LLM to generate Meridian visuals, include:

> Meridian is open-source public-good software for volunteer operations. It should feel field-ready, communal, cooperative, warm, useful, and trustworthy. The brand uses a soft hexagonal field emblem with a central hub and radiating paths, representing coordinated staff contribution around a shared source of truth. It should avoid military, police, emergency-service, corporate SaaS, startup, compass rose, shield, map pin, and generic monogram imagery. Use an earth + signal palette with canvas neutrals, charcoal, slate, sage, dust tan, and signal amber.

## 17. Migration From Previous Style Guide

The previous style guide described Meridian with a circular navigation/globe motif, sweeping directional path, North Star reference, and blue/cyan/teal primary palette. That direction should be considered superseded by this guide.

Carry forward:

- Volunteer Operations Platform positioning.
- Calm, reliable, organized, trustworthy, human-centered brand attributes.
- Avoidance of militaristic, tactical, emergency-only, corporate, social, and gaming-inspired styling.
- Inter as the primary UI typeface.
- Lucide-style rounded outline icons.
- Clear operational UI personality.

Replace:

- Circular navigation/globe logo concept.
- North Star motif.
- Blue/cyan/teal primary identity.
- Overly navigation-heavy visual language.
